How to Recreate Your Custom GPT Using MCP Factor

An 8-step guide to rebuilding your Ai assistant with more deployment options, business integrations, and control over how it operates.

OpenAI is retiring Custom GPTs on December 11, 2026. If you've invested time building one, that doesn't mean your instructions, knowledge, or business logic need to go to waste.

You can recreate much of that functionality (and then some!) using MCP Factor,

A true custom agentic solution.

Ready?

Step 1. Collect Your Custom GPT's Components

Before doing anything, open your existing GPT in ChatGPT and review its configuration.

Preserve the following:

  • Instructions: Your system prompt, rules, personality, restrictions, and response formatting.
  • Knowledge: Uploaded PDFs, documents, reference files, and FAQs.
  • Conversation starters: Examples of the tasks your GPT was designed to perform.
  • Custom Actions: API endpoints, authentication requirements, and OpenAPI schemas.
  • Test prompts: Five to ten questions you can use to evaluate behavior.

Copy everything you have permission to reuse into a separate document.

Important: Your previous GPT conversations and custom Actions will not automatically transfer to MCP Factor.

Step 2. Create Your Agent

Visit app.mcpfactor.com and sign in or create an account. It’s free!

  1. Open Agents from the dashboard.
  2. Create a new agent.
  3. Give it a name and select its timezone.
  4. Open Agent Settings → Prompt Engineering.
  5. Paste your Custom GPT's system instructions.
  6. Configure the welcome message, personality, and behavior.
  7. Save your configuration.

You can also use the Describe your agent feature to generate an initial configuration from a plain-English description.

Your existing prompt is a starting point, not a guarantee of identical behavior. Test and refine it.

Step 3. Choose Your Ai Model

Your Custom GPT used OpenAI's models. MCP Factor supports multiple model providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, xAI Grok, and Google Gemini.

Within your agent's model settings:

  1. Choose your preferred provider and model.
  2. Configure the required API credentials or supported billing arrangement.
  3. Adjust available model settings for your use case.
  4. Test the same prompts against your original GPT.

You can continue using OpenAI models without requiring your customers to interact through the ChatGPT interface. Plus you can select your backup models in case the primary model becomes unavailable!

Model availability, pricing, and supported features vary by provider.

Step 4. Restore Your Knowledge Base

Navigate to:

Agent Settings → Prompt Engineering → Knowledge Base

Upload the reference files you preserved from your Custom GPT.

Supported native formats include PDF, TXT, Markdown, HTML, JSON, CSV, XLSX, and XLS.

The documented native upload limits are:

  • 25 files per agent
  • 50 MB maximum per file
  • 500 MB combined
  • Up to 10 files per upload

Wait until the documents finish indexing before testing. Upload completion doesn't necessarily mean the knowledge is ready.

Ask questions that require information from the uploaded documents and check that the answers are accurate.

Step 5. Rebuild Your Custom Actions as Functions

This is where you can move beyond a conversational assistant.

If your Custom GPT used Actions to interact with external services, you'll need to recreate those integrations.

Navigate to:

Tools → Functions → + New

MCP Factor functions can support actions such as:

  • Capturing and updating customer information
  • Syncing data with Google Sheets
  • Sending webhooks and notifications
  • Triggering automation flows
  • Updating contact fields and tags
  • Initiating follow-up sequences
  • And more..

Create your function, define its inputs and actions, then assign it to the appropriate agent.

For API integrations, verify the endpoint, authentication, permissions, request structure, and expected responses.

Never assume an old GPT Action will work without adaptation and testing.

Step 6. Connect Business Workflows

Your recreated agent can become part of a larger business process.

Depending on your configuration, you can connect it to CRM pipelines, payment systems, booking calendars, voice services, messaging channels, and mobile wallet pass workflows.

For example, instead of merely explaining a loyalty program, your agent could be configured to:

  1. Identify an existing customer.
  2. Retrieve permitted customer information.
  3. Check loyalty or membership records.
  4. Trigger an eligible offer or wallet-pass workflow.
  5. Update the contact record.
  6. Initiate an appropriate follow-up.

The actual functions require the corresponding integrations, authorization, and workflow configuration.

Step 7. Deploy Your Agent

Open your agent's Channels tab.

Under Web Chat, you'll find options for sharing or embedding your agent.

You can:

  • Share a direct chat link.
  • Add a chat launcher to your website.
  • Embed the agent within a webpage.
  • Connect supported messaging channels.

Can you smell what we’re cooking?

Customize the appearance under Agent Settings → Appearance to match your brand.

Unlike a legacy Custom GPT, a website-deployed MCP Factor agent does not require customers to open ChatGPT to interact with it. True freedom!

Step 8. Test Before Going Live

Before publishing, run a basic quality assurance checklist.

  • Does the agent follow its system instructions?
  • Can it accurately retrieve information from its knowledge base?
  • Does it handle unknown questions without inventing answers?
  • Do its functions trigger correctly?
  • Does customer information reach the intended destination?
  • Are authentication, consent, and permissions functioning correctly?
  • Does the website or messaging deployment work?
  • Are conversation failures and edge cases handled appropriately?

What About Ownership?

Push your creation to GitHub. Find that integration under Settings.

Keep copies of your prompts and knowledge files, understand where customer data is stored, and verify available export and migration options.

The goal isn't to eliminate every vendor dependency. It's to build a system whose critical business components, customer relationships, and operational processes you can manage deliberately.

Ready to Rebuild?

You don't necessarily need to start over because OpenAI is retiring Custom GPTs.

Preserve what you've built, recreate its core functionality, and use the opportunity to design something that does more than answer questions.

Your Ai assistant can become part of a functioning business system.
